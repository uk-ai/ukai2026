**Theme:** Towards Pluralistic AI

AI is conventionally developed under a positivist "scientific" approach that values objectivity and certainty - for example models are evaluated against objective benchmarks, deviations from which are assumed to be errors. In contrast, artistic thinking as practiced by artists and reflected more broadly across the humanities adopts a subjectivist and interpretivist stance that values plurality, subjectivity and ambiguity, and where deviation from norms is surfaced and celebrated.

This panel will argue for a more pluralistic approach to AI. Why should AI embrace ambiguity and subjectivity, and how might it approach this? What benefits might this bring to the human experience of AI, including recognising and celebrating marginalised perspectives? And how can we negotiate the resulting tensions between transparency and ambiguity, and between bias and subjective interpretation?

The panel brings together speakers to offer distinct perspectives on these challenges, illustrating them with ongoing research from the UKRI Turing AI Fellowship "Somabotics: Creatively Embodying AI":

- **Prof Steve Benford** (Chair) - framing the challenge
- **Dr Kieran Woodward** - the AI perspective
- **Dr Richard Ramchurn** - the artist's perspective
- **Dr Stacy Hsueh** - the HCI perspective
